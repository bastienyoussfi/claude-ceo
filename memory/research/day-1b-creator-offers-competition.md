## C-2 — Podcast show notes service (~€25/episode)

### A. Competition: the AI-SaaS field is dense and cheap

Current pricing of the incumbent self-serve tools:

| Tool | Price | Included | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Castmagic | $39/mo Hobby (300 min), $59/mo Starter (800 min); ~$19–179/mo annual | transcripts, show notes, social posts, blog drafts | [MED] https://hackceleration.com/labs/castmagic-pricing, https://tekpon.com/software/castmagic/pricing/ |
| Podsqueeze | $18/mo Starter (160 min), $39/mo Pro (320 min); $11–27/mo annual | show notes, timestamps, newsletters, clips, one-click | [MED] https://aichief.com/ai-entertainment-tools/podsqueeze-ai/, https://sourceforge.net/software/product/Podsqueeze/ |
| Capsho | ~$29/mo Podcaster (4 episodes) to $99/mo Entrepreneur; one source says "starts ~$69/mo" — conflicting, pricing page blocked our fetch (403) | full promo suite: show notes, emails, captions, blog | [LOW — unresolved conflict] https://www.schoolofpodcasting.com/is-capsho-worth-it/, https://www.creatoreconomytools.com/tool/capsho, https://www.capsho.com/pricing |
| Swell AI | free Hobby; $29/mo Studio; $49/mo Agency; credits from ~$8.99/hr | show notes generator, articles, social | [MED] https://tooldirectory.ai/tools/swell-ai-podcast-content-automation, https://www.swellai.com/ai-podcast-show-notes |
| ShowScriber (budget end) | ~$6–7 one-time per use | transcript + notes + chapters | [LOW] https://alternativeto.net/software/showscriber/about |

**The math a podcaster sees:** a weekly show at €25/episode pays us ~€100/mo. The same podcaster gets Podsqueeze for $18/mo or Castmagic for $39/mo. **We are 3–5x the SaaS price, not cheaper.** The €25 price is only "cheap" against the *human* anchor ($50–150/episode, per the morning file) — so the offer only works if it is sold as a replacement for a human VA/writer, never as a tool alternative.

**What buyers complain about with the SaaS tools (our opening):**
- Output is generic, lacks the host's voice; users report spending **30–60 minutes editing every batch, "which defeats the purpose of using a tool"** [LOW–MED — appears in a competitor's comparison page, so discount accordingly, but matches the pattern across sources] https://www.castmagic.io/software-comparisons/castmagic-vs-podsqueeze, https://www.castmagic.io/software-reviews/podsqueeze
- Limited tone/depth customization; manual tweaking expected [LOW] https://www.castmagic.io/software-reviews/podsqueeze
- Independent head-to-head testing of show-notes generators exists as a genre ("Show notes generators tested — which…"), i.e. quality dissatisfaction is a live topic [LOW] https://thepodcasttechstack.substack.com/p/show-notes-generators-tested-which
- Fiverr humans run $5–80/gig for podcast notes — there is also a cheap-human tier below the $50–150 pro rate [LOW] https://block.fiverr.com/gigs/podcast-notes

**Honest answer to the critical question:** No, €25/episode is not cheaper than self-serve SaaS; it is only better if the deliverable is *finished* (zero editing, in the host's voice, delivered without the podcaster touching a dashboard). That "done-for-you, not do-it-yourself-with-AI" wedge is real (the 30–60 min editing complaint) but narrower than the morning framing implied.

### B. Distribution

- **RSS scraping for contact emails:** technically viable and widespread — commercial scrapers exist openly (Apify podcast directory scraper: https://apify.com/ryanclinton/podcast-directory-scraper; CastFox guide: https://www.castfox.net/blog/how-to-find-podcast-contact-emails). Scraping public data is legal (hiQ precedent, per https://soundsprofitable.com/podcast/data-scraping-is-officially-legal-5-other-stories-for-april-22-2022/), but **use** of the emails must comply with spam law [MED].
- **Counter-signal:** podcasters are already spam-fatigued from exactly this — hosts (Transistor, RSS.com) now offer email-hiding *because* of RSS scrapers (https://transistor.fm/changelog/hide-email/, https://rss.com/blog/protecting-rss-com-podcasters-from-email-spam/, https://podnews.net/article/podcasts-rss-spam) [HIGH — first-party host announcements]. Cold outreach must be low-volume, genuinely personalized (free sample of notes for *their latest episode*), or it lands in a hostile inbox.
- **EU spam-law one-liner:** B2B cold email is legal under legitimate interest in France/NL/Ireland/Sweden with ID + opt-out, but **Germany, Spain, Italy, Austria, Belgium, Poland require opt-in even for B2B** — target permissive countries or English-speaking markets, always with opt-out [MED] https://overloop.com/blog/cold-email-illegal, https://iscoldemaillegal.com/blog/gdpr-cold-email-compliance/.
- **Communities:** r/podcasting-type subreddits require weeks of genuine participation before promo and gate low-karma accounts [LOW–MED] https://www.thepodcasthost.com/promotion/reddit-for-podcasters/, https://dev.to/sh20raj/reddit-self-promotion-framework-how-to-post-smart-and-stay-unbanned-1kfg. Facebook podcast groups require human accounts — Operator-dependent. **Practical channel: personalized free-sample email at small volume, Operator-sent.**

### C. Unit economics

€100/mo = **4 episodes = one weekly-show customer**. Fulfillment per episode (transcript → edited notes in host's voice): plausibly 30–60 min AI+operator. Comfortable at €25/episode; a single retained weekly customer clears the bar. Best fulfillment-to-price ratio of the three.

### Verdict — C-2: **WEAKER than this morning, but alive.**
The morning file priced us against humans ($50–150); the real anchor is $18–39/mo SaaS that every podcaster has already heard of, making us 3–5x the incumbent price. The offer survives only as "finished, voice-matched, zero-editing notes" sold to podcasters who tried the tools and hated the editing burden — a narrower but documented segment. Distribution is workable (public RSS emails + free-sample personalization, permissive-country/US targeting) but the channel is pre-poisoned by spammers. One weekly customer = €100/mo keeps the economics attractive.

## C-3 — Etsy listing rewrite packs (~€29/10 listings)

### A. Competition: undercut from below AND absorbed by the platform

| Tool | Price | Included | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Etsy itself (built-in, free)** | free | AI listing-title suggestions + bulk apply, attribute suggestions in Search Visibility dashboard, Writing Assistant; rolled out Fall 2025 | [HIGH — Etsy Seller Handbook + trade press] https://www.etsy.com/seller-handbook/article/1404283886419, https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2025/09/05/etsy-adds-new-ai-tools-sellers-marketplace/, https://www.valueaddedresource.net/etsy-guidance-listing-titles-ai/ |
| ListifyAI | free 5 listings; $7/mo annual / $9/mo for **50 listings** | GPT-4o vision: title + description + 13 tags from a photo, 10 seconds | [MED — vendor] https://www.listifyai.net/ |
| eRank | $5.99–29.99/mo | keyword/SEO tooling + AI features | [MED] https://listsgenie.com/etsy-seo-tools-comparison |
| Alura | ~$19.99/mo annual | keywords, research, listing helper | [MED] https://listsgenie.com/etsy-seo-tools-comparison |
| ListsGenie / BetterListing | Chrome-extension tier | photo → full listing | [LOW] https://alternativeto.net/software/listsgenie/about, https://alternativeto.net/software/betterlisting/about |

**The kill shot:** €29 for 10 listings vs ListifyAI's $7–9/mo for 50 listings — we'd be ~20x the per-listing price of a tool doing the identical deliverable — and vs **Etsy's own free, native, bulk-apply AI title/attribute tool** that Etsy actively nudges sellers toward [HIGH]. Unlike podcasting there is no strong "human rate" anchor to undercut. The morning pain evidence ("listing hell", 30–45 min/listing) is real but the market has already been flooded with cheap and free answers to it.

**Complaint texture:** Etsy sellers' AI hostility runs the other way — the community backlash is about AI junk *on* Etsy and mandatory AI disclosure in listings (https://techcrunch.com/2024/07/09/etsy-new-seller-policy-2024-generative-ai) [MED]. Pitching "AI-written listings" into that culture is headwind, not tailwind.

### B. Distribution

- **Etsy official forum: closed.** Community Policy prohibits unsolicited promotion by outside services and repeated offsite links; the update effective 2026-04-30 prohibits promotional content outright [HIGH] https://www.etsy.com/legal/community/, https://www.etsy.com/legal/policy/community-policy-effective-until-april/1474779337736, and CindyLouWho2's summary https://www.patreon.com/posts/changes-to-etsys-154645593.
- **Off-Etsy:** Etsy-seller Facebook groups exist in volume (lists of 20+: https://millennialboss.com/etsy-seller-facebook-groups/, https://multiplegroupposter.com/blog/facebook-groups-for-etsy-sellers/) — promo rules vary per group (promo days, some ban links) [LOW–MED], and all require a human account → fully Operator-dependent. No permissionless channel found.

### C. Unit economics

€100/mo = ~3.5 packs of 10 listings. Fulfillment per pack (10 listings × title + 13 tags + description with real keyword checking): 1–2 h AI+operator. Economics fine *if* buyers existed — the problem is price anchoring, not fulfillment.

### Verdict — C-3: **WEAKER — effectively dead.**
This morning the case rested on "sellers pay for tools and still hand-write listings." This afternoon's finding: Etsy shipped free native AI listing tools (Fall 2025) and third parties sell the exact deliverable at $7–9/mo for 50 listings. A €29/10 pack is ~20x overpriced against a direct substitute, the community culture is AI-hostile, and the only official forum bans promotion outright. Recommend PARK/DEAD at the Day 2 decision unless repositioned into something the tools don't do (e.g. full shop audits — but that's a different candidate).

## C-5 — Google review response drafting for local SMBs (€29/mo)

### A. Competition: the price umbrella is real but the floor is $0

| Option | Price | Notes | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Birdeye | $299–449/mo per location + hidden fees (real first-year $4–6k) | enterprise reputation suite | [MED] https://costbench.com/software/review-management/birdeye/, https://www.reviewflowz.com/blog/how-much-does-birdeye-really-cost |
| Thryv | ~$228–244/mo entry, up to $929/mo | full SMB marketing suite w/ AI responder | [MED] https://schedulingkit.com/pricing-guides/thryv-pricing, https://zeeg.me/en/blog/post/thryv-pricing |
| **ReplyOnTheFly** | **$0 free plan (unlimited AI drafts, 5 direct posts/mo); $9.99/mo Pro** | email-first: AI draft lands in inbox, one-tap approve/post — *exactly our proposed workflow, productized* | [MED — vendor's own site] https://www.replyonthefly.com/, https://www.replyonthefly.com/blog/best-ai-review-tools |
| ChatGPT / Claude direct | free | "best free option for SMBs willing to copy-paste"; Zapier/IFTTT recipes automate it end-to-end | [MED] https://www.ainstein.blog/playbooks/ai-respond-to-google-reviews, https://zapier.com/apps/google-my-business/integrations/google-my-business/1706567/respond-to-google-my-business-reviews-with-chatgpt, https://ifttt.com/applets/yHBGXKuS-automatically-use-chatgpt-to-respond-to-new-google-my-business-reviews |

**Does free ChatGPT kill the paid service?** Not by itself — the documented pain is "don't know what to say" (33%) and "too time-consuming" (18%) (morning source: https://www.replyonthefly.com/blog/google-review-response-time-study — note this is ReplyOnTheFly's own study [LOW–MED]); owners who won't copy-paste into ChatGPT exist. **But ReplyOnTheFly does:** it packages the identical draft-and-approve loop at $0–9.99/mo. €29/mo must therefore buy something beyond drafts — e.g. we monitor, draft, chase the owner, and post, i.e. full concierge — and even then the reference price a diligent buyer finds is $9.99, not $299. Google confirms AI-drafted replies are acceptable practice [MED] https://www.rightresponseai.com/blog/can-i-use-ai-to-respond-to-google-or-other-reviews.

### B. Distribution

- **Mechanic:** scrape Google Maps for businesses with unanswered reviews → personalized cold email containing a ready-to-post draft response to their most recent negative review → €29/mo payment link. The free-sample hook remains the best part of this candidate.
- **Volume math [MED benchmarks, LOW precision]:** agency-to-SMB cold email replies ~4.2%; SMB segment 4–7% (https://belkins.io/blog/cold-email-response-rates, https://www.breakcold.com/blog/cold-email-reply-rate, https://puzzleinbox.com/blog/cold-email-reply-rate-benchmarks). Assume ~5% reply, ~20% of replies convert → **~1 customer per 100 personalized emails**. Reaching €100/mo (4 customers) ≈ 400+ emails plus 1–3 follow-ups each — every send Operator-executed, and non-technical SMB owners are slow email-checkers.
- **EU legal:** same country split as C-2 — permissive in FR/NL/IE/SE with opt-out, opt-in required in DE/ES/IT/AT/BE/PL [MED] https://overloop.com/blog/cold-email-illegal. Targeting matters.

### C. Unit economics

€100/mo = **4 customers at €29/mo**. Fulfillment per customer: minutes/day (check reviews, draft, send for approval) — trivially handleable; 20+ customers feasible on AI+operator time. Fulfillment is the best of the three; **acquisition is the worst**: ~400 personalized Operator-sent emails for €100/mo of recurring revenue, against a $0–9.99 competitor a buyer can find in one search.

### Verdict — C-5: **WEAKER than this morning.**
The enterprise price umbrella ($228–449/mo) is real, but the morning file missed that the bottom of the market is already served at $0–9.99/mo by ReplyOnTheFly — whose product is literally our proposed workflow — and by free ChatGPT/Zapier recipes. €29/mo is only defensible as a full concierge ("you never think about reviews again"), and the acquisition math (~100 Operator-sent emails per customer) makes this the slowest path to first revenue of the three. Keep only if the Day 2 decision values recurring revenue over speed-to-first-euro.

## Cross-candidate summary for the Day 2 decision

1. **All three got weaker under competition pressure.** The morning files priced against humans; the real anchors are $0–39/mo AI SaaS in every case.
2. **Ranking after this pass: C-2 > C-5 > C-3.** C-2 keeps a real wedge (documented editing burden on SaaS output; one weekly customer = €100/mo). C-5 keeps a wedge (concierge, recurring) but has brutal acquisition math and a $0 competitor. C-3 is dead on price against Etsy's own free tools.
3. **Structural lesson:** in creator/seller markets, any offer whose deliverable equals "text an existing SaaS emits" must be sold as *finished work replacing a person*, never as cheaper AI — and priced against the human, to buyers who already rejected the SaaS.
4. **Distribution lesson:** no permissionless channel exists for any of the three. Everything routes through Operator-sent personalized email (legal in FR/NL/IE/SE + US with opt-out; opt-in countries excluded) or Operator-held community accounts. Channel cost must be part of the Day 2 scoring.
