## 2. ICP SELF-SERVE PARADOX — the buyer demonstrably builds, and gives it away

Direct observation beats theory here: within **five weeks** of the API shipping, a government department (MoJ), a robotics company (Shadow Robot), and two consultancies (The Software House, Xebia) each built the tool and open-sourced it. Consultancies treat it as content marketing; enterprises treat it as an afternoon platform task. That is the purchasing behavior of this ICP, observed, not hypothesized.

The honest counter-evidence: dev teams *do* pay for thin Slack layers — [Geekbot charges $2.50–3/user/mo](https://www.vendr.com/marketplace/geekbot) for standup prompts, and [Axolo](https://axolo.co/features/slack-github-pull-request-reminder)/[Eleminder](https://www.eleminder.com/) sell PR-reminder bots even though [free OSS scripts](https://github.com/ekmartin/slack-pull-reminder) and [native GitHub scheduled reminders](https://docs.github.com/en/subscriptions-and-notifications/concepts/scheduled-reminders) exist. But the survivors share three things C-10 lacks: human-in-the-loop workflows with daily engagement (not read-only metric relays), years of reviews and Slack-marketplace distribution, and per-user pricing on a habit. And the category's most famous specimen is the warning, not the encouragement: **Pull Panda** sold Slack reminders over the GitHub API as a subscription — [GitHub acquired it in 2019 and made it free](https://github.blog/news-insights/company-news/github-acquires-pull-panda/) ([TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/github-acquires-pull-panda-and-makes-its-code-review-tools-available-for-free)), then shipped scheduled reminders natively. A read-only convenience relay in the platform's own monetization path gets absorbed; that is the base case.

## 3. COMPETITOR / NATIVE CLOSURE — the gap is shrinking monthly, from both sides

- **SessionWatcher** has NOT closed the org gap (still macOS, individual, [$2.99–59 one-time](https://www.sessionwatcher.com/copilot); its [comparison page](https://sessionwatcher.com/guides/best-copilot-usage-trackers), updated July 2026, still shows no team/Slack column) — but it ships burn-rate projection + overage warnings for individuals, and its answer for teams is "DIY".
- **Native, individual:** [Visual Studio 2026 now ships a real-time Copilot cost tracker with proactive approaching-limit alerts in the IDE](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/319403/20260630/visual-studio-2026-fixes-copilot-billing-blind-spot-native-cost-tracker.htm) (2026-06-30). [Individuals see credits per billing cycle in settings since 2026-07-20](https://github.blog/changelog/2026-07-20-copilot-users-can-now-see-ai-credits-used-per-billing-cycle/).
- **Native, org:** [budgets with 75/90/100% email alerts](https://docs.github.com/en/billing/concepts/budgets-and-alerts) + user-scoped hard caps (per `c10-native-coverage-check.md`), and [full budget lifecycle APIs went GA 2026-06-04](https://github.blog/changelog/2026-06-04-budget-and-usage-management-apis-now-generally-available/) — which mostly makes DIY easier. Slack delivery is still absent natively (verified: docs list UI + email only), so the literal wedge exists today — but GitHub has shipped a billing-visibility feature roughly **every two weeks** since June, already has [a GitHub app for Slack](https://docs.github.com/en/integrations/how-tos/slack/use-github-in-slack), and D-004 pre-registered "gap closes" as a kill trigger. This gap's remaining life is plausibly measured in weeks, and we'd be renting it.
- **UsageBox** is not the competitor Day 1 thought: it is an [AI-billing-infrastructure platform](https://usagebox.com/) farming these keywords with SEO articles ([example](https://usagebox.com/articles/github-ai-credits-tracking-usage-api-2026)) — which itself shows the content shelf around this pain is already staffed by better-funded writers.

## 4. WILLINGNESS TO PAY — no comparable found that proves €19/mo/org for this

Searched for any surviving paid product whose whole offer is "platform usage numbers relayed to Slack": found none independent — the survivors are either absorbed (Pull Panda), workflow tools (Geekbot, Axolo), or full analytics platforms. The economics don't rescue it either: the buyer's alternative is not €0-value suffering, it's (a) free native email alerts they already have, (b) a free 632-star dashboard, or (c) one afternoon from a developer they already employ — whose output they can then keep forever, fed by a growing pile of MIT-licensed references. €228/yr against that alternative set, sold by an anonymous 2-day-old account with zero reviews, contradicts every trust-rail lesson in `adversarial-summary.md` (§3). Nothing found this session proves anyone pays a third party a recurring fee for Copilot credit alerts; four orgs were found doing the opposite.

## VERDICT: KILL

**Fatal blow: the entire product already exists as free, maintained, open-source software — a 632-star semi-official dashboard showing per-user `ai_credits_used`, a public GitHub-Action-to-Slack bot, and in-house builds by the exact buyer (the UK Ministry of Justice shipped its own MIT-licensed version within five weeks of the API) — while our own launch comment publishes the complete build recipe and tells readers it works without us.**

The Operator's attack holds on both prongs: the individual-alert half is native (GitHub budgets/email, per-cycle settings view, VS 2026 in-IDE warnings), and the team half is a one-afternoon build the ICP demonstrably performs and open-sources rather than buys. The residual wedge (pace projection → Slack for non-billing-admin team leads) is real but is a ~50-line cron script sitting in GitHub's active monthly shipping path, with Pull Panda as the canonical fate of paid thin relays over the GitHub API. No comparable was found proving recurring payment for this shape, four counterexamples were found disproving it, and the validation channels cannot plausibly deliver 10 signals by 2026-08-02 — so the remaining "let the gate decide" option yields no information at nonzero cost. Kill now; promote C-12 (Apify paid Actor) per D-004's own fallback clause rather than spending the week's content budget marketing a foregone failure.

Survivability note (a *different* idea, not a fix): what the evidence says people pay for in this space is either (a) deep attribution the API hides — per-model/per-repo/per-feature cost, the gap [UsageBox's content names](https://usagebox.com/articles/github-ai-credits-tracking-usage-api-2026) — which is a real product with a real moat and far exceeds our €20/mo build constraint, or (b) engagement workflows with humans in the loop (the Geekbot pattern), which alerting is not. A relay of numbers from a free API to Slack is the one shape here with no defensible layer at any price.
