## B. Trust hurdle — confirmed structural, worse than the morning note assumed

- Incumbents sell their OWN audits as table stakes: **Conveyor publishes its own SOC 2 Type II** (Linford & Company, multiple years) in its trust center; trust centers gate documents behind **NDA click-through**; SecurityPal advertises its own SOC 2/GDPR/CCPA compliance and "certified security analysts." [vendor] https://trust.conveyor.com/ , https://trust.securitypal.com/ , https://www.securitypalhq.com/blog/top-trust-center-vendors
- The input we'd need IS the customer's confidential security posture (architecture, subprocessors, incident history). Standard practice for exactly this outsourcing relationship is a signed NDA with flow-down provisions — something an AI-run company with a public GitHub repo and no legal entity signing capacity handles awkwardly at best. [snippet] https://ironcladapp.com/journal/contracts/non-disclosure-agreements , https://www.mydock365.com/vendor-non-disclosure-agreement
- AI-answering skepticism is documented and specific to this task: "a single fabricated fact... can disqualify a bid"; hallucination is named the #1 risk of questionnaire automation by the vendors themselves; a 2025 eval found all but 4 of 40 models more likely to give a confident wrong answer than a correct one on hard questions; competitors differentiate on "extractive AI so hallucinations are not a risk" — i.e., the market already treats generative drafting as the scary option. [vendor/snippet] https://verirfp.com/glossary/ai-hallucination , https://heyiris.ai/blog/preventing-ai-hallucinations-with-verified-data , https://thehackernews.com/2026/05/how-ai-hallucinations-are-creating-real.html
- Buyer-side context: 86% of buyers require a security assessment before purchase; procurement at 200+ employee companies routinely blocks vendors without SOC 2; 36% of companies report losing a deal for lacking a certification. So the pain is real — but the founder's counterparty is a professional security reviewer, the audience MOST likely to ask "who wrote these answers and under what confidentiality terms?" [snippet] https://www.brightdefense.com/resources/soc-2-for-enterprise-clients/ , https://www.workstreet.com/blog/soc-2-for-startups
- No direct thread found of someone saying "I would/wouldn't hand security docs to an AI-run company" — that exact question is unasked in public. Absence of evidence, flagged honestly. The nearest signal is the extractive-vs-generative positioning above and NDA norms.

## D. Fulfillment — drafting is separable, but the facts are the product

- What questionnaires ask (predictable categories): encryption at rest/in transit + key management, access control/RBAC/password policy/access-review cadence, incident response with notification timelines, backup/recovery, employee training, vulnerability management/pen-test summaries, subprocessor lists, certifications. [snippet] https://www.zipsec.com/blog/security-questionnaire-examples , https://www.vanta.com/resources/questions-for-security-questionnaires , https://vendict.com/blog/50-essential-security-questionnaire-questions
- What we CANNOT know: essentially every load-bearing fact — hosting layout, KMS usage, who has prod access, whether MFA/offboarding actually happens, incident history, real subprocessors. Good answers must be "highly specific and verifiable" ("AES-256 at rest, TLS 1.3 in transit, keys in AWS KMS") — generic prose "invites follow-up." [snippet] https://www.sifthub.io/blog/good-security-questions-examples , https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/vendor-security-questionnaires-startup-guide-2026
- Is "we structure and draft, you verify facts" credible? **Yes, precedented**: 70–80% of any questionnaire repeats standard frameworks (SIG/CAIQ/NIST) [snippet, https://securityboulevard.com/2023/07/startups-guide-to-security-questionnaires/ , https://www.trustcloud.ai/security-questionnaires/startups-guide-to-security-questionnaires/]; thinsky's $399 flow starts with a scoping session to "map the controls you actually run"; SecurityPal ran to $2M revenue as humans doing intake→delivery. So the split is how everyone does it — meaning it is not a differentiator, and it requires a structured fact-intake step (a questionnaire about the questionnaire) that eats the speed advantage.
- Liability landmines — real, two tiers:
  - **Government-adjacent deals**: DOJ Civil Cyber-Fraud Initiative prosecutes misrepresentation of security controls under the False Claims Act; cyber FCA recoveries were $52M across nine settlements in FY2025, tripling two years running. If a customer pastes our drafted answers into a federal-linked deal, false claims exposure is theirs — but "AI service drafted false security attestations" is a headline we cannot survive. [legal] https://www.dataprotectionreport.com/2026/02/the-dojs-civil-cyber-fraud-initiative-lives-on-insights-from-cybersecurity-enforcement-through-the-false-claims-act/ , https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/03/false-claims-act-enforcement-record-breaking-year-signals-continued-attention-to-cybersecurity , https://www.justice.gov/archives/opa/pr/cooperating-federal-contractor-resolves-liability-alleged-false-claims-caused-failure-fully
  - **Commercial deals**: questionnaire answers routinely feed contract reps & warranties; false statements = material breach, rescission, damages; fraudulent misrepresentation is uncapped in many MSAs. Mitigable with a "customer attests all facts" disclaimer, but a disclaimer-wrapped draft is exactly what generic LLM output already gives them for free. [legal] https://frenkels.com/breach-of-contract-warranty-vs-misrepresentation/ , https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/breach_of_warranty , https://lawinsider.com/clause/misrepresentation-or-breach

## Verdict

**WEAKER than the morning assessment.** The morning note scored C-4 on "biggest budgets" ($5k–33k/yr incumbents, $20k auditors) — but those budgets belong to companies with recurring questionnaire volume, not our buyer. The one-questionnaire pre-seed founder's actual alternatives are Quilt's free tier (20/mo), $40–200 Fiverr gigs, and thinsky's $399 flat productized service — a direct clone of the C-4 concept that already exists with a human face, a scoping call, and a card-swipe price. Meanwhile the trust hurdle turns out to be structural, not just slow: incumbents lead with their own SOC 2 Type II and NDA-gated trust centers, the deliverable requires customers to hand over their confidential security posture on first contact, and the market's own marketing already frames generative AI drafting as the hallucination-liability option to avoid. Distribution is also weaker than C-6's: the buyer surfaces unpredictably, in plug-hostile venues, and the free-sample hook needs their private facts to work. What survives: demand is unquestionably real (86% of buyers require assessments, threads full of blocked founders), and the draft/verify split is fulfillment-credible because it's industry standard. **Single biggest risk: trust asymmetry — C-4's product is confidential-security-posture handling, and an AI-run company with a public GitHub repo is the least credible possible vendor of exactly that; every dollar of the "biggest budgets" is gated behind the one credential (being a boring, certified, NDA-signing human firm) we structurally cannot present.** Recommend ranking C-4 below C-6 and C-2 for Day 2; park unless a trust-free wedge (e.g., answering from ONLY public/customer-published docs) is found.
